Kansas Soil Water Atlas

Daily plant available water in the top 60 cm (2 ft) of Kansas soils since 2002, mapped at 1-km resolution, with the timing, extent, and duration of soil water deficits and surpluses.

Soil Water Processes Lab, Department of Agronomy, Kansas State University · Daily data from Jan. 1, 2002 to %%LAST_DAY%% · Updated %%BUILT%%

All maps and charts in this atlas are based on the fraction of plant available water (FAW, unitless) in the top 60 cm (2 ft) of the soil profile, estimated daily on a 1-km grid across Kansas. A value of FAW = 0 corresponds to the permanent wilting point (i.e., the water content below which plants can no longer extract water from the soil), and FAW = 1 corresponds to field capacity (i.e., the amount of water a soil holds after excess water has drained, typically about one day after the soil has been thoroughly wetted at or near saturation).

To account for both the magnitude and the duration of dry and wet conditions, we integrated FAW over time in units of FAW-days. Each day with FAW below 0.5 adds its shortfall (i.e., 0.5 − FAW) to the annual deficit-days, and each day with FAW above 0.5 adds its excess (i.e., FAW − 0.5) to the annual surplus-days. For instance, 10 days at FAW = 0.3 and 40 days at FAW = 0.45 both result in 2 deficit-days.

Annual summaries

Each annual summary includes the polar chart of daily statewide median FAW, maps of the annual deficit-days and surplus-days, and maps of the monthly mean FAW. Summaries are available for complete calendar years only.

About the dataHow the atlas is made

FAW was obtained from the Kansas 1-km soil moisture model that supports the daily map. The model consists of a daily soil water balance driven by PRISM rainfall and a seasonal drying rate calibrated with Kansas Mesonet observations. Soil water limits were derived from USDA-SSURGO soil texture, and the model is adjusted toward the Kansas Mesonet stations each day. The record starts on Jan. 1, 2002, and the entire record is rerun whenever the model changes.

Statewide values are area-weighted averages of the 273,579 pixels in Kansas. Weeks are fixed 7-day periods starting on Jan. 1 (week 52 also includes Dec. 30–31), and years are calendar years. Deficit-days and surplus-days use FAW = 0.5 as the reference. Stored water was computed as FAW multiplied by the plant available water capacity of each pixel (i.e., the water held between field capacity at −10 kPa and the permanent wilting point) and summed across the state.
